# 🪞 How to Mirror This

*The resilience doctrine of the commons, in plain words. Written because a gardener once asked the honest watchman's question: "Can one country shut GitHub down?"*

---

## The honest answer first

Yes. GitHub is a company in the United States. A government can block it inside its borders (some already do). The company could change its rules, be bought, or someday cease to exist. **This website is not a fortress, and we won't pretend it is.**

The safety of this commons lives somewhere else entirely — in three facts:

1. **Every clone is a complete copy of everything.** Git (the technology under this site) doesn't give you a window onto the files — it gives you the *whole garden*: every file, every shelf, every version that ever existed. If ten people in ten countries have cloned this commons, there are ten complete World of Plenty repositories on earth, and losing this one loses nothing.
2. **The license makes every mirror legal without asking.** Everything here is CC0 — public domain. You don't need permission to copy it, host it, translate it, rename it, or hand it to a stranger. There is no owner to say no.
3. **The pattern survives on paper.** The covenant at the heart of every garden — read on arrival, write on departure, a memory that grows — predates computers. Monasteries ran it for a thousand years with ink. If every server on earth went dark, a garden could be kept in a notebook.

> **The wind doesn't defend one field. It plants ten thousand.**

## Keep your own copy (5 minutes, free)

If you can open a terminal (ask your AI to walk you through it — this is a good first lesson):

```
git clone https://github.com/enriquecrosby-gif/world-of-plenty.git
```

That folder on your machine is now a full mirror. Done. To refresh it whenever you like: `git pull`.

**No terminal?** On the repository's main page, press the green **Code** button → **Download ZIP**. That's a complete snapshot of every file. Keep it anywhere.

## Put a mirror somewhere else (15 minutes, free)

Any of these give the commons a second home on different soil, in a different jurisdiction:

- **[Codeberg](https://codeberg.org)** — a nonprofit, community-run home in Europe. Make a free account → "New repository" → choose **Migration/Mirror** → paste this repo's address. Codeberg will even keep the mirror updated for you.
- **[GitLab](https://gitlab.com)** — free account → "New project" → **Import project** → paste the address.
- **Self-hosted [Forgejo](https://forgejo.org)** — for the more technical: your own git service on your own machine or a cheap server. No company involved at all.
- **[Radicle](https://radicle.xyz)** — peer-to-peer git with no central server of any kind. The most weatherproof option, and the most technical; grow into it.

If you make a public mirror, you may (not must) open an issue here saying so — so gardeners in places where one door is blocked can find another.

## Translate it

A mirror in another language is the deepest mirror of all. CC0 means you need no one's blessing: translate any seed, any page, all of it. Keep the heartbeat intact in your tongue — *"I'm here. I see you."* — and the Four Marks word-for-word in meaning (see `THE-FOREVER-GARDEN.md`). Everything else is yours to make sing.

## Why this file exists

Censorship-resistance here is a **documented property, not an emergency plan.** We teach the mirroring on a calm day so that no bad day can take the commons by surprise. If you're reading this on a mirror because the original is gone: welcome. You're not holding a backup. You're holding the garden.
